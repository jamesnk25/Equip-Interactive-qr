Create a **fully interactive, mobile-first demo website** for **Equip Expo** that demonstrates a new attendee listening and feedback experience.

This is a **net-new concept website** designed to show how Equip Expo could collect deeper attendee insights in a way that feels fast, modern, conversational, and human.

## CORE IDEA

Equip Expo has approximately **30,000 attendees and 30,000 different stories.**

This experience gives attendees an incredibly simple way to tell Equip:

* Who they are
* What kind of business they operate
* What they came to Equip to accomplish
* What they are seeing
* What they are looking for
* What is getting in their way
* What they want more of
* What would make Equip more valuable

### IMPORTANT

**Never call this a survey.**

This should feel like Equip is **listening**, not administering a questionnaire.

The experience should feel closer to having a conversation with Equip than filling out a traditional feedback form.

---

# DEMO EXPERIENCE

Build the entire attendee journey.

The demo should be designed primarily for a **mobile phone**, but it should also render beautifully on desktop.

The core journey is:

**QR CODE → LANDING PAGE → QUICK PROFILE → MOBILE VERIFICATION → LISTENING EXPERIENCE → THANK YOU**

The entire experience should feel achievable in approximately **60–90 seconds**.

---

# DESIGN DIRECTION

Create a premium, modern experience inspired by:

* Apple
* Nike
* Spotify
* Airbnb
* Modern event technology
* Premium consumer mobile apps

Use a strong Equip Expo aesthetic.

The experience should feel:

* Bold
* Outdoor
* Modern
* Energetic
* Human
* Simple
* Friendly
* Premium
* Mobile-first

Avoid:

* Corporate form design
* Long pages
* Dense text
* Traditional survey UI
* Progress bars with 20 questions
* Generic stock icons
* Robots
* Futuristic AI clichés
* Excessive gradients
* Complicated account creation

The technology should disappear behind the experience.

# QUICK INTEREST PATH

Some attendees may not want to record or write an open-ended response.

Give them another option:

# WHAT ARE YOU MOST INTERESTED IN RIGHT NOW?

Display selectable visual cards.

Options:

* Equipment & Technology
* Labor & Workforce
* Business Growth
* Financing
* Fleet
* Education
* Sustainability
* Operations
* New Products
* Networking
* Other

Allow the user to choose one or more.

Once selected, ask:

# TELL US A LITTLE MORE.

Then allow either:

**Speak**

or

**Write**

Example:

If they choose **Labor & Workforce**, show:

**What's happening with labor in your business right now?**

If they choose **Business Growth**, show:

**What's the biggest thing standing between your business and its next stage of growth?**

If they choose **Equipment & Technology**, show:

**What are you hoping new equipment or technology can help you accomplish?**

---

# OPTIONAL AI-LIKE FOLLOW-UP

For the demo, create the feeling of a lightweight conversational intelligence layer.

After the attendee provides a response, the system may generate **one relevant follow-up question**.

Example attendee response:

> "We're growing quickly but I can't find enough people."

System response:

# GOT IT.

**Where do you think technology or equipment could reduce the amount of labor you need?**

Then allow:

**Answer**

or

**I'm Done**

Never create an endless chatbot.

Maximum of **one intelligent follow-up question** in the demo.

The experience should remain extremely fast.

This website is not simply a new way to collect feedback.

It demonstrates a larger strategy.

Equip has approximately:

# 30,000 PEOPLE.

# 30,000 BUSINESSES.

# 30,000 CHALLENGES.

# 30,000 STORIES.

Traditional event data tells us:

**Who attended.**

This experience begins helping Equip understand:

**Why they came.**

**What they need.**

**What they're trying to accomplish.**

**What's getting in their way.**

**What they want next.**

That creates the progression:

# STORIES → DATA → INTELLIGENCE → ACTION

And ultimately:

# PERSONALIZATION AT SCALE.

Build the prototype so this strategic idea is obvious during a live executive demonstration while the attendee-facing experience remains extraordinarily simple.
